Selected work

Research

My dissertation studies consumer spending responses to economic shocks and public policies across space and time.

01

Urban economics · Consumer behavior

Spatial Spending Shifts during COVID-19: Evidence from Transaction Data in U.S. Cities

Uses large-scale transaction data and difference-in-differences methods to quantify persistent shifts in consumer spending within U.S. metropolitan areas after COVID-19.

Difference-in-DifferencesSpatial DataTransactions
Research focus

The project examines how spending moved between central business districts and surrounding neighborhoods, and how patterns varied across cities and income groups.

02

Public economics · Local markets

Temporary Tax Relief and Consumer Demand: Evidence from U.S. Sales Tax Holidays

Evaluates how temporary sales tax holidays affect high-frequency consumer spending, with a focus on geographic heterogeneity and cross-border shopping.

Event StudyHigh-Frequency DataPolicy Evaluation
Research focus

The analysis uses fine spatial cells and distance to treated-state borders to show how policy effects vary across local markets.

03

Monetary economics · Consumption

Monetary Policy Transmission to Consumption: Evidence from Transaction-Level Data

Examines the dynamic response of consumer spending to monetary policy surprises using high-frequency transaction data and local projections.

Local ProjectionsMonetary ShocksTime Series
Research focus

The project traces spending responses across horizons and studies whether positive and negative monetary policy surprises transmit differently.
